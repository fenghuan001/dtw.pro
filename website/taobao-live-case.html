01 — The Brief

Thirty livestreams, thirty fan communities

Background only. Everything from "My Contribution" onwards is my own output.

For Double 11, Taobao Live's 上播大明星 IP placed celebrities into brand livestreams across the promotional period, each carrying a co-branded 星动礼盒 gift box and its own "11"-themed challenge.

The campaign ran on two tracks at once: fan communities collecting and trading the gift boxes, and a B-side credibility play — a celebrity value ranking published with DT商业观察 to build the IP's standing with brands.

To C — fan engagementThree escalating layers of fan participation: collect the box, open it publicly, then customise it.
To B — brand influenceA trade-facing ranking across influence, sales pull and content performance, issued under a third-party name.

Each artist was given an "11" challenge so the same campaign mechanic read differently in every community — 王鹤棣空气投篮11投11中, 虞书欣一场直播被夸了11次, 檀健次11秒拿捏美拉德风格香, 赵露思11句土味情话.

03 — My Contribution / The Workflow

Producing personalised copy at scale

This is the part of the campaign worth asking about. Thirty artists, roughly twenty fan groups each, three weeks — and a fan group can tell within one line whether the copy was written for them.

Stage 01
30 × ~20

Map the audience

Thirty artists, each with its own cluster of fan groups — and each group with its own register. The roster was mapped first: who the copy was for, what they already knew, and what they would post back.

→
Stage 02
436 lines

Batch-generate

First drafts produced at volume, seeded with each artist's fixed facts — livestream date and time, brand, gift-box contents and challenge title — so nothing had to be looked up twice.

→
Stage 03
100% read

Edit and tone-check

Every line read, rewritten or approved before release. The generated draft was the starting point, never the deliverable — accuracy, tone and brand-safety were all mine to carry.

AI produced the volume. I produced the judgement. Nothing went out without a human read.

The working rule that made a single writer viable on a thirty-artist roster.
Why volume was the problem

Three artists' fan-group sets plus the platform-wide set came to 436 lines in one document — and that was a fraction of the roster.

Why it had to be parallel

Thirty livestreams ran inside a three-week window. There was no sequence to write them in; they all had to exist at once.

Why tone is the whole job

A fan community reads generic brand copy as spam. The same announcement had to sound like it was written by someone inside that group.

04 — Campaign Context / Announcement System

The announcement system the campaign ran on

Every artist on the roster got a co-branded poster — 天猫双11 × 淘宝直播 × brand, with the artist's own "11" challenge and livestream slot. This artwork and the grid were produced and supplied by the client. It is shown here as the context my part of the campaign sat inside, not as my design.

The poster system. Every announcement carried the same four elements: the co-branded lock-up, the artist's "11" challenge, the brand and ambassador title, and the livestream date and slot. Consistency across thirty artists is what let one sheet carry the whole roster.

The full roster sheet

All thirty posters, assembled into one grid — the version released and re-shared by fan accounts as a single image. It worked as a scale statement as much as an announcement: one look told you how large the roster was.

It appears on this page as context rather than as my work. My part of this campaign was the community copy and the highlight scripts written alongside this system.
